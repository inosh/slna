International Nurses Day

International Nurses Day is observed every year on 12th May, the anniversary of the birth of Florence Nightingale in 1820 — the British nurse, statistician, and social reformer widely regarded as the founder of modern, evidence-based nursing. The International Council of Nurses (ICN), the global federation SLNA belongs to, began marking the day annually in 1965, and it was formally established under the name "International Nurses Day" in January 1974.

  • 12 May 1820: Florence Nightingale is born in Florence, Italy, to a wealthy English family
  • 1854-1856: Nightingale leads a team of nurses caring for wounded soldiers during the Crimean War, dramatically reducing mortality rates through hygiene reform, and earning her the enduring name "The Lady with the Lamp"
  • 1860: She founds the Nightingale Training School at St Thomas' Hospital in London — widely considered the first secular, professional nursing school in the world
  • 1899: The International Council of Nurses (ICN) itself is founded, becoming the world's first international organisation for health professionals
  • 1965: The ICN begins marking 12 May annually in honour of Nightingale's legacy
  • 1974: 12 May is formally designated "International Nurses Day" by the ICN
  • Today: Celebrated island-wide by SLNA with award ceremonies, public awareness campaigns, and commemorative events honouring Sri Lanka's nursing workforce

Sri Lanka's Own Nightingale Connection

Nursing in Sri Lanka has a direct historical link to the Nightingale tradition being commemorated each year. The first Nightingale-trained nurses arrived in colonial Ceylon in 1878, less than two decades after Nightingale founded her training school in London, to establish the island's earliest formal nurse training programmes. This makes International Nurses Day a particularly meaningful observance for SLNA, connecting Sri Lanka's own 147-year nursing history directly to the global tradition the day commemorates.
