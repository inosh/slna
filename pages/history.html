History

The history of professional nursing in Sri Lanka spans nearly one and a half centuries, evolving from colonial-era training programmes into today's nationally organised profession supported by both a professional association (SLNA) and a statutory regulatory council.

  • 1878: The first Nightingale-trained British nurses arrive in colonial Ceylon to establish formal nurse training in island hospitals, introducing what was then called "scientific nursing"
  • 1928: The Ceylon Medical Ordinance is amended to formally recognise nurses' professional representation, providing for elected representatives on the advisory board governing medical, dental, and nursing practice
  • 1943: The Sri Lanka Nurses' Association is founded under the motto "Unity for Quality," consolidating the national voice of the nursing profession
  • 1949: The Ceylon Medical Ordinance No. 10 of 1949 provides for the registration of nurses under the Ceylon Medical Council
  • 1988: The Sri Lanka Nurses Council is established as an independent statutory body by Act No. 19 of 1988, taking over responsibility for nurse registration, education standards, and professional discipline from the Medical Council
  • 2005: The Sri Lanka Nurses Council Act is amended by Act No. 35 of 2005, updating the Council's structure and powers
  • Present day: SLNA continues as a full member of the International Council of Nurses (ICN), representing Sri Lankan nurses within a global federation of over 140 national nursing associations

Two Distinct Institutions

It's worth understanding the difference between SLNA and the Sri Lanka Nurses Council, since both play important but separate roles. SLNA, founded in 1943, is a professional association — its work centres on advocacy, continuous professional development, and representing nurses collectively. The Sri Lanka Nurses Council, established later in 1988, is the statutory regulatory body responsible for maintaining the official register of qualified nurses, setting curriculum standards, and handling professional discipline. Before 1988, nurse registration fell under the Ceylon Medical Council per the Medical Ordinance No. 10 of 1949.
