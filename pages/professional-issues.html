Professional Issues

The Sri Lanka Nurses' Association (SLNA) is committed to promoting a professional environment in which nurses are respected, supported, and enabled to provide safe, ethical, and high-quality care. We recognise that nurses work in demanding and changing settings, and that professional issues can affect both the wellbeing of nurses and the quality of care received by patients and communities.

SLNA's commitment: We provide a professional platform for nurses to raise concerns, share experience, and contribute to constructive discussion on matters affecting nursing practice and the nursing workforce.

Key Professional Issues

SLNA engages with members, health-service leaders, educational institutions, government agencies, and other stakeholders on issues that influence nursing practice, professional development, and patient safety. Key areas of concern include:

  • Safe staffing, appropriate skill mix, and fair workload allocation.
  • Professional recognition, career progression, and leadership opportunities for nurses.
  • Access to continuing professional development, education, and specialist training.
  • Safe workplaces, occupational health, and measures to prevent violence, harassment, and discrimination.
  • Ethical decision-making, professional accountability, and support for quality nursing practice.
  • The effective use of technology, digital health systems, and accurate nursing documentation.
  • Clear communication and collaboration among nurses, patients, families, and multidisciplinary healthcare teams.
  • The wellbeing, resilience, and mental health of nurses working in demanding care environments.

Supporting Quality Nursing Practice

High-quality nursing depends on a workplace culture that values clinical judgement, teamwork, learning, and respectful communication. SLNA encourages nurses to maintain professional standards, engage in lifelong learning, and contribute their knowledge to service improvement in their workplaces and communities.

We also support constructive approaches to professional concerns. Where issues arise, SLNA encourages dialogue based on evidence, mutual respect, patient safety, and the long-term interests of the nursing profession.

Professional Development and Leadership

The nursing profession continues to evolve in response to changes in healthcare needs, technology, and population health. SLNA promotes opportunities that help nurses strengthen clinical knowledge, leadership skills, research awareness, teaching capacity, and confidence in professional advocacy.

Developing nursing leadership at every level—clinical, educational, managerial, and policy-related—is important to ensuring that the perspectives of nurses inform decisions about healthcare delivery in Sri Lanka.

Raising a Professional Concern

SLNA members and nursing professionals are encouraged to share concerns, suggestions, and emerging issues that may require professional discussion or advocacy. Information submitted through SLNA will be considered respectfully and, where appropriate, may help inform future engagement, education, or policy advocacy.

Please note: SLNA does not replace formal employer, regulatory, legal, emergency, or disciplinary processes. Concerns involving immediate risk to patient safety, workplace emergencies, or formal complaints should also be reported through the appropriate authorised channels.
