Nurses' Rights & Welfare

Nurses are central to the health and wellbeing of individuals, families, and communities throughout Sri Lanka. The Sri Lanka Nurses' Association (SLNA) is committed to promoting the dignity, safety, wellbeing, and professional rights of nurses in every area of practice.

A strong health system depends on a nursing workforce that is valued, adequately supported, and able to provide care in safe and respectful working environments. SLNA advocates for conditions that enable nurses to fulfil their professional responsibilities while maintaining their own physical, emotional, and social wellbeing.

Our commitment: SLNA works to promote fair treatment, professional respect, safe practice environments, and access to support for nurses. We encourage constructive dialogue and collective action on issues that affect the welfare of the nursing profession.

Areas of Advocacy

SLNA engages with relevant stakeholders on matters that affect the rights, welfare, safety, and professional lives of nurses. Key areas include:

  • Safe staffing levels, manageable workloads, and appropriate skill mix in healthcare settings.
  • Safe, respectful, and non-discriminatory workplaces for all nurses.
  • Prevention of workplace violence, harassment, intimidation, and bullying.
  • Access to adequate equipment, resources, and support needed to deliver safe patient care.
  • Fair opportunities for professional development, education, promotion, and leadership.
  • Recognition of nursing knowledge, clinical judgement, and contribution to healthcare delivery.
  • Occupational health and safety, including protection from workplace hazards and infection risks.
  • Support for the mental health, wellbeing, and resilience of nurses.
  • Work-life balance and conditions that support nurses with family and caring responsibilities.

Safe and Respectful Workplaces

Every nurse should be able to work in an environment that protects their safety, dignity, and professional standing. SLNA supports efforts to prevent violence and harassment, encourage respectful teamwork, and ensure that nurses can raise concerns without fear of unfair treatment.

Safe workplaces also support safe patient care. When nurses have adequate staffing, effective communication, appropriate resources, and access to professional support, they are better able to deliver compassionate and high-quality care.

Wellbeing and Professional Support

Nursing can be rewarding, but it can also be physically and emotionally demanding. SLNA recognises the importance of supporting nurses who work long hours, manage complex clinical responsibilities, respond to emergencies, and care for patients and families during difficult times.

We encourage nurses to seek support early when experiencing stress, fatigue, burnout, workplace conflict, or other challenges. Professional wellbeing is an essential part of maintaining a sustainable nursing workforce and safe standards of care.

Raising a Welfare Concern

Members and nursing professionals may contact SLNA to share concerns, suggestions, and experiences related to professional welfare. Information shared with the Association may help identify common issues and inform advocacy, awareness activities, professional dialogue, or engagement with relevant stakeholders.

Important: SLNA does not replace emergency reporting, employer grievance procedures, legal services, police reporting, or statutory regulatory processes. If there is immediate danger, risk to patient safety, violence, or a formal workplace complaint, please use the appropriate authorised reporting channels without delay.

Working Together for Nurses

The rights and welfare of nurses are strengthened when nurses are informed, connected, and able to speak collectively. Through membership, professional engagement, education, and advocacy, SLNA seeks to support a nursing profession that is respected, protected, and empowered to serve the people of Sri Lanka.
