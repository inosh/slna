Nursing Policy

The Sri Lanka Nurses' Association (SLNA) works to ensure that the knowledge, experience, and concerns of nurses are represented in national health policy discussions. As the professional voice of nurses, SLNA advocates for policies that strengthen nursing services, support the nursing workforce, and improve safe, equitable, and quality care for communities throughout Sri Lanka.

Our role: SLNA is a professional association that advocates for nurses and the nursing profession. Statutory registration, education standards, and professional regulation are administered by the Sri Lanka Nurses Council under the relevant legislation.

SLNA Policy Priorities

SLNA engages with the Ministry of Health, professional bodies, educational institutions, and other stakeholders on policy matters that affect nurses, patients, and the wider health system. Our priorities include:

  • Strengthening safe staffing levels, fair deployment, and workforce planning across health services.
  • Promoting professional development, continuing education, and career pathways for nurses.
  • Advocating for safe workplaces, staff welfare, occupational health, and the rights of nurses.
  • Supporting evidence-informed nursing practice, quality improvement, and patient safety.
  • Encouraging nursing leadership and meaningful nurse participation in healthcare decision-making.
  • Promoting equitable access to quality nursing care for all communities in Sri Lanka.
  • Engaging with national and international partners to advance the nursing profession.

Professional Regulation and Standards

SLNA supports strong professional standards and works constructively with the relevant statutory authorities. The Sri Lanka Nurses Council was established under the Sri Lanka Nurses Council Act, No. 19 of 1988, later amended by Act No. 35 of 2005. The Council has responsibility for areas including the statutory registration of qualified nurses, nursing education standards, professional conduct, and disciplinary processes.

Before the establishment of the Sri Lanka Nurses Council, nurse registration was administered under the Ceylon Medical Ordinance No. 10 of 1949 through the Ceylon Medical Council. Today, the Council maintains the official register of nurses and undertakes functions related to professional standards and recognition of qualifications.

Working With Stakeholders

Effective nursing policy requires collaboration. SLNA contributes the practical perspective of nurses who deliver care in hospitals, community settings, educational institutions, and specialised services. We seek constructive dialogue with government agencies, health-service leaders, educators, civil-society organisations, and international nursing partners.

Through advocacy, consultation, professional dialogue, and continuing development activities, SLNA aims to help shape a health system in which nurses are supported, respected, and equipped to provide the highest possible standard of care.

Share Your Perspective

Nurses, members, and stakeholders are encouraged to share policy issues, professional concerns, and constructive suggestions with SLNA. Your experience helps inform advocacy priorities and supports a stronger voice for nursing in Sri Lanka.
